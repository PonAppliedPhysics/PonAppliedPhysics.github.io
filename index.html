Multidisciplinary Capstone · Honda

BEV Thermal Runaway Detection

Overview

As Honda transitions its Marysville Auto Plant to full BEV production, our six-person multidisciplinary team evaluated four sensor technologies for early thermal runaway detection on 800-lb battery packs — too heavy to evacuate manually. Conducted laboratory validation experiments and delivered deployment recommendations under a $3,500 budget.

Detection Technologies

  • Infrared Pyrometer (Optris CS LT) — Verified temps above 41°C at up to 3 ft; accuracy degraded beyond 4.5 ft
  • Thermal MEMS (Omron D6T-32L) — Accurate heating curves at ~4 samples/sec; sensitivity limited by reflective surfaces
  • HF Gas Sensor — Detected 6.6 ppm in 6.5s at 0.5m; CV under 4% for timing across all distances
  • Ultrasonic Transducer (Olympus V103-SB) — ANOVA confirmed significant voltage trends with charge state (p = 1.91×10⁻⁴ charging; p = 4.17×10⁻⁶⁰ discharging)

My Contributions

  • Participated in the September 2024 on-site visit to Honda's Marysville Auto Plant and contributed to stakeholder interviews
  • Led background research on thermal runaway causes, global BEV market trends, and existing detection methods
  • Conducted concept screening using a weighted user-needs matrix, narrowing from 10 technologies to 4
  • Designed and executed ultrasonic transducer validation experiments including MATLAB data acquisition and ANOVA testing
  • Built the Python data analytics pipeline for post-processing all four sensor experiments

Result

Delivered full validation dataset, sensor selection matrix, and layered multi-sensor deployment strategy to Honda's BEV engineering team. Completed at $1,268 — 36% of the $3,500 budget.

Machine Learning · Physics 5680

Weather Forecasting with ML

Overview

Built an end-to-end machine learning pipeline to predict daily weather variables for Columbus, Ohio, using 50+ years of historical observations from John Glenn Columbus International Airport (NOAA). Engineered 12 temporal features and compared Ridge Regression against Random Forest.

Results

  • Random Forest R² = 0.82 for next-day TMAX prediction (vs. 0.77 for Ridge Regression)
  • TMAX_roll7 dominated feature importance at 0.669 — 66.9% of predictive weight
  • Precipitation remained difficult to predict from surface observations alone
  • Full pipeline runs in under 30 seconds on a commodity laptop

Methodology

Dataset comprised daily TMAX, TMIN, TAVG, and PRCP records supplemented with real-time feeds from the NOAA Climate Data API and OpenWeatherMap. A strictly chronological train/test split prevented data leakage.

Experimental Physics · Physics 5700

Saturated Absorption Spectroscopy of Rubidium

Overview

Resolved the hyperfine structure of the 5²P₃/₂ excited states of ⁸⁵Rb and ⁸⁷Rb using saturated absorption spectroscopy, overcoming ~500 MHz Doppler broadening. A tunable external-cavity diode laser near 780.2 nm was swept across the D2 transition line of natural rubidium vapor.

Key Results

  • ⁸⁷Rb ground splitting: 6.65 ± 0.3 GHz (accepted: 6.835 GHz, 2.7% difference)
  • ⁸⁵Rb ground splitting: 3.0 ± 0.2 GHz (accepted: 3.036 GHz, 1.2% difference)
  • Interferometer FSR: 429 ± 4 MHz per fringe
  • Achieved sub-Doppler resolution of ~10 MHz against a 500 MHz background
  • Both real hyperfine transitions and crossover peaks resolved for all four ground-state transitions

Methodology

Counter-propagating pump and probe beams addressed zero-velocity atoms at exact resonance, producing narrow Lamb dips (~10 MHz). An unequal-arm Michelson interferometer with ΔL = 0.35 m provided absolute frequency calibration.

Computational Physics · Physics 5700

Thermal Conductivity Modeling — PINNs

Overview

Modeled 1D heat diffusion along a 0.75-inch-diameter, 84.25 cm copper rod instrumented with eleven Type-T thermocouples. Developed five progressively refined models and validated each against two independent experimental runs using chi-squared analysis.

Model Progression

  • Model 0 (Analytic): Closed-form Fourier series — fails during heating phase
  • Model 1 (FD): FTCS finite-difference — same BC limitation
  • Model 2/2b (FD): Data-driven boundary conditions — 10×+ chi-squared reduction
  • Model 3 (Optimized): SciPy-optimized radial heat loss — best overall
  • PINN (TensorFlow): Recovered thermal diffusivity as a learnable parameter (MAE = 0.42°C)

Key Achievements

  • Built a PINN that recovered thermal diffusivity without manual tuning (Raissi et al. 2019)
  • Vectorized NumPy cut runtimes from minutes to ~3 seconds
  • Identified and corrected errors in published reference documentation
